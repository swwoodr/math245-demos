📐 The models and their terminal speeds ▼

Take up as positive. Newton's second law with gravity and a drag force opposing motion gives $m\,\dfrac{dv}{dt} = -mg + F_R$. Dividing by $m$ and writing $\rho = k/m$:

Linear drag ($F_R = -kv$, slow or small objects): $\dfrac{dv}{dt} = -\rho v - g$. This is linear first-order, so

$$v(t) = \left(v_0 + \frac{g}{\rho}\right)e^{-\rho t} - \frac{g}{\rho}, \qquad v_\tau = \frac{g}{\rho}.$$

Quadratic drag ($F_R = -kv|v|$, faster objects): $\dfrac{dv}{dt} = -g - \rho v|v|$. It is separable on each leg: going up, $v' = -g - \rho v^2$; coming down, $v' = -g + \rho v^2$. Setting $v' = 0$ on the way down gives

$$v_\tau = \sqrt{g/\rho}.$$

Either way, a falling object's speed levels off at the terminal speed $v_\tau$, where drag balances weight. Notice that with drag the trip down takes longer than the trip up.
