📐 Why complex numbers show up in differential equations ▼

The characteristic equation of $y'' + 2y' + 5y = 0$ is $r^2 + 2r + 5 = 0$, whose roots are $r = -1 \pm 2i$ — a complex-conjugate pair. Complex roots of a real polynomial always come in conjugate pairs, which is why $\bar z$ matters.

Multiplying adds angles and multiplies lengths. In particular, multiplying by $i$ rotates by $90^\circ$ (try $w = i$), and multiplying by $e^{i\theta}$ rotates by $\theta$. That rotation is where the $\cos$ and $\sin$ in oscillating solutions come from.
