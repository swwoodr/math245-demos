📐 From complex roots to real solutions ▼

If the characteristic equation of $ay'' + by' + cy = 0$ has roots $r = \alpha \pm \beta i$, then $e^{(\alpha + \beta i)t}$ and $e^{(\alpha - \beta i)t}$ both solve the ODE. They are complex, but averaging them cancels the imaginary parts:

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\left(e^{(\alpha+\beta i)t} + e^{(\alpha-\beta i)t}\right) = e^{\alpha t}\cos\beta t, \qquad \tfrac{1}{2i}\left(e^{(\alpha+\beta i)t} - e^{(\alpha-\beta i)t}\right) = e^{\alpha t}\sin\beta t.$$

So the real general solution is $y = e^{\alpha t}(c_1\cos\beta t + c_2\sin\beta t)$: $\beta$ sets how fast it oscillates and $\alpha$ sets whether it decays ($\alpha < 0$), grows ($\alpha > 0$), or keeps a constant amplitude ($\alpha = 0$). Turn on the conjugate to see the two rotating points whose average is the real part.
