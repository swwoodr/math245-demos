📐 Deriving the rocket equation ▼

Newton's second law for a body that is losing mass: exhaust leaves at speed $c$ relative to the rocket, so over a short time $\Delta t$ the momentum balance gives $m\,\dfrac{dv}{dt} = -c\,\dfrac{dm}{dt} + F_{\text{ext}}$. With a constant burn rate, $\dfrac{dm}{dt} = -\beta$, $m(t) = m_0 - \beta t$, and the thrust is $\beta c$.

No drag, no gravity: $\dfrac{dv}{dt} = \dfrac{\beta c}{m_0 - \beta t}$ integrates to the Tsiolkovsky rocket equation

$$v(t) = v_0 + c\ln\frac{m_0}{m(t)}, \qquad \Delta v = c\ln\frac{m_0}{m_f}.$$

With gravity (still no drag), subtract $g t$: the burnout speed is $v_b = c\ln\dfrac{m_0}{m_f} - g\,t_b$ with $t_b = (m_0 - m_f)/\beta$. After burnout the rocket coasts with constant mass $m_f$.

With drag $k\,v|v|$ there is no closed form, so we solve numerically — exactly the kind of problem where Euler, Improved Euler, and ode45 are compared. Because the thrust switches off abruptly at burnout, the flight is solved in two pieces: the burn on $[0, t_b]$, then the coast starting from the burnout state (in MATLAB, two ode45 calls).

Simplifications: constant $g$ and constant air density, launched straight up from rest. See the ODE Solvers demo for the methods themselves.
