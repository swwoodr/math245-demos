📐 The small-angle approximation ▼

For small angles, $\sin\theta \approx \theta$, which turns the pendulum equation into a linear, constant-coefficient second-order ODE — the same form as a spring-mass-damper:

$$\theta'' + c\,\theta' + \omega_0^2\,\theta = 0, \qquad \omega_0 = \sqrt{g/L}.$$

With $c = 0$ its solution is $\theta(t) = \theta_0\cos(\omega_0 t)$, with period $T_0 = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$ — the same for every release angle.

The true (nonlinear) period does depend on amplitude:

$$T = 4\sqrt{\tfrac{L}{g}}\;K\!\left(\sin\tfrac{\theta_0}{2}\right) \approx T_0\left(1 + \tfrac{\theta_0^2}{16}\right),$$

where $K$ is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind. The linear model is within 1% up to about $\theta_0 \approx 23^\circ$; as $\theta_0 \to 180^\circ$ the period grows without bound. Watch the two pendulums drift out of phase at large angles.
