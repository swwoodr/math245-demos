📐 Where the threshold R₀ = 1 comes from ▼

Since $S + I + R = N$ is constant, the behavior is captured by the $(S, I)$ system. At any disease-free point $(S_0, 0)$ the Jacobian is upper triangular, so its eigenvalues can be read off the diagonal:

$$J(S_0, 0) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -\beta S_0/N \\ 0 & \beta S_0/N - (\gamma + q)\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \lambda_2 = \frac{\beta S_0}{N} - (\gamma + q).$$

A small outbreak grows exactly when $\lambda_2 > 0$, i.e. when $\mathcal{R}_e = \dfrac{\beta}{\gamma + q}\cdot\dfrac{S_0}{N} > 1$. With everyone susceptible this is the basic reproduction number $\mathcal{R}_0 = \beta/(\gamma + q)$.

Vaccination lowers $S_0/N$; the epidemic cannot take off once the vaccinated fraction exceeds $1 - 1/\mathcal{R}_0$ (herd immunity). Quarantine raises the removal rate from $\gamma$ to $\gamma + q$, lowering $\mathcal{R}_0$ itself. Either one can push $\lambda_2$ below zero.

In the SIRS model, immunity wanes, susceptibles are replenished, and when $\mathcal{R}_0 > 1$ the disease settles at an endemic equilibrium instead of dying out.
